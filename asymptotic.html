  • GO assumes a local plane wave. Each ray carries amplitude, phase and polarization. A wall multiplies the ray by ΓTE or ΓTM.
  • Image method: mirror the source in each wall to get exact paths. The cost grows as (walls)reflections. Ray launching (SBR): shoot many rays and catch them in a sphere at Rx. The cost grows with the number of rays.
  • GO gives zero field in the shadow. Set Reflections to 0 and move Rx behind a building. Real fields are not zero there: that is diffraction.

ΓTE = (cosθ − √(εc − sin²θ)) / (cosθ + √(εc − sin²θ))  ·  ΓTM = (εccosθ − √(εc − sin²θ)) / (εccosθ + √(εc − sin²θ))  ·  εc = εr − jσ/(ωε0)

Top view: the walls are tall and the rays stay in the horizontal plane (2.5-D), so the field spreads as 1/r. TE: the scalar field is vertical E. TM: the scalar field is vertical H (ΓTM is the H ratio). Walls are opaque here; real tools also add transmission through walls. Materials: ITU-R P.2040-3, Table 3 (now superseded by P.2040-4). Its frequency ranges are indicative: most materials are fitted from 1 GHz, brick to 10 GHz, ground 1–10 GHz only, so 900 MHz and 28 GHz values are extrapolated.

Diffraction fills the shadow

At an edge, one incident ray makes a cone of diffracted rays. UTD gives each diffracted ray its amplitude.

ISB: incident shadow boundary. RSB: reflection shadow boundary. GO jumps at both. UTD adds a diffracted field that cancels each jump. At the ISB the total is about 6 dB below the incident field: the ISB term gives exactly half the field; the other wedge terms add a little, most when the ISB is near the RSB. Keller's GTD is infinite at the boundaries; the UTD transition function F removes the singularity.

Ed = Ei(Q) · D(φ, φ′, L, n) · √(s′ / (s (s + s′))) · e−jks  ·  D: Kouyoumjian–Pathak (1974), lossy faces after Luebbers (1984)

Physical optics: currents from the tangent plane

PO does not trace the field to the observer. It puts the current of an infinite flat mirror on each lit point, then radiates that current.

Js = 2 n̂ × Hinc on lit faces  ·  Js = 0 in the shadow

2-D strip, TMz (E parallel to the edges), PEC. MoM: EFIE, pulse basis, 20 cells/λ, solved in your browser. PTD adds the fringe field of each edge: Keller's half-plane coefficient minus the PO edge term.

σ2DPO = k w² cos²θ · sinc²(k w sinθ)

3-D plate a × b, rotating in the plane of side a: σPO = (4πA²/λ²) cos²θ · sinc²(k a sinθ)

  • Near specular, PO is correct. The peak k w² matches MoM within 0.1 dB.
  • Away from specular, edges dominate. PO gives deep false nulls and the wrong level. Near edge-on it is far too low.
  • PTD corrects the edges. PO + PTD follows MoM within a fraction of a dB here. Real tools add PTD or MEC on every wedge.

Rayleigh region (ka < 0.5): PO scales as (ka)², not 9(ka)⁴; the error is 10 dB at ka = 0.1. Resonance region: the creeping wave interferes with the specular return. Optical region: PO is within about 1 dB for ka > 10 and about 0.4 dB for ka > 20; both tend to πa². Mie ripples because of the creeping wave, which PO does not have. PO ripples a little too, from its abrupt shadow boundary.

Shooting and bouncing rays

SBR shoots a dense grid of GO rays at the target. Each ray bounces. At each hit, a PO integral over the ray tube gives that ray's share of the scattered field.

  • One bounce is PO with shadowing. It gives the two plate flashes of the dihedral, but not its wide return.
  • Two bounces add the double reflection. The 90° dihedral retro-reflects over ±45°: σ2D = 2k w² on the bisector, falling as the aperture 2w sin(45° − |φ|) shrinks. SBR follows MoM within about 1 dB over ±30°. Near ±40°, edge diffraction (not in SBR) gives errors of 2–3 dB.
  • SBR has no edges. Production codes add PTD or MEC for wedges and UTD for creeping waves (Ling, Chou & Lee 1989; Xpatch).

2-D, TMz, PEC, plates 5λ long, 10 rays per λ. Tools on SBR: Ansys HFSS SBR+ (SBR with PTD and UTD edge corrections and creeping waves), CST Studio Suite Asymptotic Solver (SBR). Remcom Wireless InSite X3D shoots and bounces rays for propagation, then moves each path to the exact receiver point. Remcom XGtd uses GO, UTD, PO and MEC. Sionna RT uses SBR to find candidate paths, then the image method.

Planning models: statistics instead of geometry

Empirical models fit curves to measured path loss. They need no building map. They give a median and a spread, not a field.

f in MHz, d in km, heights in m. Hata: 150–1500 MHz, hb 30–200 m, hm 1–10 m, d 1–20 km. COST-231 Hata: 1500–2000 MHz, constant 46.3 + 33.9 log f, plus 3 dB in large-city centres. Large-city a(hm) is defined for f ≤ 200 and f ≥ 400 MHz; between them this page interpolates. Drag on the plot to move d.

  • Two-ray: past the breakpoint the direct and ground rays cancel, and the loss grows 40 dB per decade instead of 20.
  • Hata grows about 30–35 dB per decade over its valid hb of 30–200 m (slope 44.9 − 6.55 log hb). Real signals scatter about the median: lognormal shadowing, σ ≈ 6–10 dB.
